## Summary

- Claims checked: 118 (every number in the text, tables and captions, plus the dated facts and the cross-report comparisons): 76 confirmed, 35 corrected, 7 not verifiable from here.
- All corrections were made at the source, in `report/build_logistics.py` (hand-entered constants `EXTERNAL` and `THRESHOLDS`, and the text blocks), then the report was regenerated (Markdown, HTML and the four SVG charts). No data file was touched. The script runs with 0 warnings.
- The most consequential correction is the **Suez share**. The 63% of `transit-times.json` is Alphaliner's figure for all Far East–Europe services (5.50 of 8.70 million TEU, 15 September–15 October 2026); for **Far East–North Europe** Alphaliner gives **58%**, for the Mediterranean 70%. The report applied 63% to a Shanghai–Rotterdam voyage. With 58% the weighted voyage is 34.0 days (not 33.5) and the cost stack 842 $/FEU (not 832); the rise since 2023 is +74% (not +72%). The share of the freight-rate rise explained by costs stays at 14%.
- **2023 baselines** (priority checks): both values are right, but the WCI link pointed to the wrong week. Singapore VLSFO, October 2023 average, 668.50 $/t (Ship & Bunker via PortNews, 16 November 2023): confirmed in a search excerpt. Drewry WCI Shanghai–Rotterdam 1,048 $/FEU on 2 November 2023: confirmed in two reprints; the cited Hellenic Shipping News page «World Container Index up 7% this week» is the **9 November 2023** assessment (1,272 $/FEU). Link replaced. Neither page could be opened from the review session (the hosts are outside its network allowlist), so both stay «reliability medium».
- **Hull value 150 vs 221.7 M$**: resolved, not an error. 150 M$ is the transit model's hypothesis for an in-service ship; 221.7 M$ is the newbuild price of a 15,500 TEU ship (`assicurazioni.json`, used by the Northern Europe report). The 0.32% break-even in `assicurazioni.json` differs from this report's 1.09% mostly because of the cost base (UNCTAD 0.7 M$, mid-size ship, 2024 fuel, no carbon cost, against 1.64 M$ here: ×2.3), and only partly because of the hull (×1.5). With this model's cost on a 221.7 M$ hull the line is 0.74% (0.29–0.51% with the toll). The report now says so in section 3.
- **Cape break-even 0.43–1.09%**: arithmetic correct. The logic was incomplete: charter hire of the 10.5 extra days is excluded, so the band is a lower bound and the assessment «above about 0.5% would make the Cape competitive for most large ships» overstated it. Reworded.

Method: every figure recomputed with Python directly from `docs/data/*.json` (independent of the script, then compared with the script's variables); cost stack, waterfall, break-even and scenario costs recomputed by hand. External claims checked with web search on 10 November 2026; WebFetch to portnews.ru, airfreight.news, hellenicshippingnews.com and shipandbunker.com failed (hosts not resolvable from the session), so those checks rest on search-result excerpts.

## 2. Consistency with the other reports and the dashboard

- **Northern Europe report** (`docs/report/en/northern-europe-2026-11.html`): its Asia–Rotterdam cost stack uses a 4,000 t last leg and prices one Red Sea war-risk premium on the 221.7 M$ newbuild (0.49 M€, 0.40–0.59). This report uses 3,540 t (Singapore → Rotterdam at 150 t/day) and 150 M$ (0.38 M$). Both are declared hypotheses; the difference is now stated in section 3 and in the limits.
- **Italian supply-chain report** (`report/supply-chain-2026-11.md`) and the **simulator** use UNCTAD's 0.7 M$ (about 160 $/FEU) for the Cape detour; this model gives 257 $/FEU in fuel and carbon. Stated in section 8 and in the limits.
- **Hormuz and Red Sea report**: same 63% Alphaliner figure, quoted for Far East–Europe; consistent. Its WCI figures (4,389 composite) match `noli.json`.
- **`rischio.json`**: the Red Sea container route is rated **high** (18.0/25, probability 5.0). The report's opening assessment («a cost problem more than a capacity problem») did not mention it; it now does. Scenario signals A–C match the dashboard; scenario E now uses the dashboard's Baltic «worse» signals.
- **`port-risk.json`**: all scores, levels and trends match. `assicurazioni.json` counts one attack within 350 km of Bab el-Mandeb in 30 days; `eventi.json` shows strikes on land in Yemen (6–7 November), none on ships, so the dashboard's «0 attacks on ships» is consistent.
- **Assessments**: all paragraphs of reasoning are marked *Assessment*. Key point 5 (what to watch) is now labelled as an assessment; the causal sentence in key point 3 was removed.
