1. Key points

  • Rotterdam's traffic is flat on the year; the weekly drop is noise. From 1 January to 30 October 2026, Rotterdam averaged 82.7 ship calls a day, −0.5% on the same days of 2025; Antwerp-Bruges −6.4%. The −10.8% in Rotterdam's latest week is noise. The clear fall is at Russia's oil port of Primorsk, −52% (IMF PortWatch).
  • German and Dutch gas storage is far below normal before winter. On 8 November 2026 Germany was 58.9% full and the Netherlands 63.5%, against an EU goal of 90%. Germany is 15.9 points below a year ago (GIE AGSI+). The TTF gas price is 82.33 €/MWh, +164% on a year.
  • Offshore wind will reach under half of the North Sea goal for 2030. Europe connected only 1.96 GW in 2025. For Denmark, Germany, the Netherlands and Belgium WindEurope expects 31.1 GW in 2030, 48% of the 65 GW promised at Esbjerg.
  • Carbon rules now add a fifth to a half of fuel costs. At the EU auction price of 86.91 €/t (9 November 2026), the EU ETS costs 275 € per tonne of fuel burnt on voyages between EU ports and 138 € on voyages to or from outside the EU (bunker-emissioni.json).
  • The Gulf of Finland is where sabotage risk concentrates. In the AIS snapshot of 9 November 2026, 44 of the 109 ships seen there were on Western sanction lists. Since November 2024 six Baltic cable incidents have been recorded. GPS jamming hit 214–246 areas a day in the week to 8 November 2026 (GPSJam).

What the numbers say. Over the year to date 6 of the ten ports are within ±5% of 2025. The biggest falls are Ust-Luga (−12.8%) and St Petersburg (−10.7%); the biggest rise is Szczecin-Świnoujście (+4.2%). The weekly figures swing much more: Rotterdam is −10.8% on the week to 30 October 2026 but +2.3% on 30 days. Calls count ships, not cargo: a port can handle fewer, larger ships.

Containers moved east and to deep water in 2025. Rotterdam handled 14.24 million TEU (+3.1%), Hamburg 8.3 million (+7.3%), Wilhelmshaven 1.47 million (+74%) and Gdańsk 2.77 million (+23%), according to the port authorities (porti-nord.json). TEU is the 20-foot container unit.

Russia's Baltic oil ports. Primorsk averaged 1.3 calls a day this year against 2.7 in 2025 (−52%); Ust-Luga −12.8%. Ukrainian drones hit both ports on 23 and 29 March and again in July 2026 (baltico-sicurezza.json). PortWatch does not see ships with AIS switched off, so Russian ports are undercounted.

AssessmentThree forces drive the pattern. First, the return of some Asia–Europe services to Suez (transits +10.3% on a year, portwatch.json) adds calls in the Mediterranean before the North Sea, which flattens Rotterdam and Antwerp. Second, the Baltic is redistributing: Polish ports hold or gain, ports that lived on Russian transit (HaminaKotka −17%, Riga −5% year to date) lose. Third, energy: tanker calls follow the oil and LNG trade, and Rotterdam lost bunker sales (−23.2% tonnes in the first nine months of 2026, Port of Rotterdam via bunker-emissioni.json). These are links we draw; the data do not prove causation.

TTF price

The TTF, the Dutch hub price that sets gas prices for most of Europe, closed at 82.33 €/MWh on 9 November 2026 (front-month future, provisional close): −3.8% on the day, +1.1% on a month and +164% on a year. US Henry Hub was 3.203 $/MMBtu. Converted at 1.1225 $/€, TTF equals 27.1 $/MMBtu: the transatlantic gap of about 24 $/MMBtu is what pulls US cargoes to Europe (calculation by this script; prezzi.json, Yahoo Finance).

Power links

North Sea countries trade power through subsea direct-current (HVDC) cables: NorNed (700 MW) and COBRAcable (700 MW) from the Netherlands, Viking Link (1.4 GW, Denmark–UK) and NordLink (1.4 GW, Norway–Germany) (esposizione-nord.json). OpenStreetMap shows these HVDC export and interconnector cables under construction: Bornholm–Germany, Eastern Green Link 1, Eastern Green Link 2, IJmuiden Ver Alpha, IJmuiden Ver Beta, IJmuiden Ver Gamma, Nederwiek 1, NeuConnect (rete-elettrica.json). In the Baltic, Estlink 2 (650 MW, Estonia–Finland) was cut by the anchor of the Eagle S on 25 December 2024 and restored in June 2025 (interconnessioni.json).

AssessmentThe winter risk for North-West Europe is storage, not supply routes. With German storage 30 points below its five-year average and most LNG coming from the US, the TTF depends on two things a port or energy company cannot control: winter temperatures and the Qatar outage linked to the Strait of Hormuz (rated «critical» on the monitor's rischio.json).

The pattern is clear in the data: rounds where the developer pays the state for the site failed in Denmark (3 GW, December 2024), Germany (2.5 GW, August 2025) and the Netherlands (1 GW, October 2025), with no bids. Rounds with a two-sided contract for difference (CfD), where the state guarantees a fixed price and pays or collects the difference with the market, worked: the UK awarded 8.4 GW in January 2026, Poland 3.4 GW in December 2025 and Denmark 1.8 GW in August 2026, signed on 6 November 2026. Next: the Dutch IJmuiden Ver Gamma-A and Gamma-B rounds (2 GW) take applications from 26 November to 10 December 2026 (RVO, secondary source).

Bottlenecks

  • Auctions and cost of capital — strain: high. offshore capacity in failed negative-bidding rounds, 2024–2025: 6.5 GW (WindEurope, 2025 Statistics, February 2026).
  • HVDC cables — strain: high. Prysmian transmission order backlog: 16.5 billion € (Prysmian, comunicato Q3 2026, 29 October 2026).
  • HVDC converter platforms — strain: high. new in-service date of Amprion's BalWin1 (2 GW) grid link: 15 September 2031 (HVDC World, 8 July 2026).
  • Turbines — strain: medium. Siemens Gamesa loss before special items, Q2 of financial year 2026: -44 million € (Renewables Now, May 2026).
  • Vessels — strain: medium. Western installation vessels (WTIV) for turbines of 15 MW or more by 2028, more than: 25 (Intelatus via Offshore Engineer, 1 May 2026).
  • Ports — strain: medium. investment announced since 2022 in factories, ports and vessels: 15.4 billion € (WindEurope, 2025 Statistics, February 2026).
  • Foundations — strain: overcapacity. European XXL monopile capacity from 2027: 2.7 million tonnes a year (Rystad Energy, 18 September 2026).

In plain terms: Prysmian holds about 16.5 billion € of transmission orders (29 October 2026) and NKT 13.0 billion (30 June 2026), so new HVDC cable capacity is booked for years; Amprion's BalWin1 2 GW grid link slipped from September 2030 to 15 September 2031 because of extra work on the offshore converter platform; European monopile factories, by contrast, will run at about 32% in 2026 and 19% in 2028 (Rystad).

Ports and vessels

Esbjerg has shipped over 27.5 GW of offshore wind in total (Port Esbjerg). Rotterdam plans a 45-hectare wind terminal for mid-2029 and Cuxhaven a 1,250-metre heavy-load quay for about 2028, while the Bremerhaven terminal was never built (eolico-filiera.json). Vessels are no longer the main constraint: the fleet of Western installation ships able to handle 15 MW turbines goes from zero in 2020 to more than 25 by 2028 (Intelatus), and Cadeler, the market leader, used its fleet at 66% in the first half of 2026 with a 2.5 billion € order book.

VLSFO = very low sulphur fuel oil, the standard marine fuel. 27 February 2026 is the last price before the war with Iran. Source: Ship & Bunker via bunker-emissioni.json (averages of quotes, not transactions).

EU ETS and FuelEU. Since 2026 shipping surrenders emission allowances (EUA) for 100% of its emissions, counted in full on voyages between EU ports and at half on voyages to or from outside the EU. At 86.91 €/t (EEX auction, 9 November 2026; 2026 auction average 78.81 €) that is 275 € per tonne of VLSFO burnt intra-EU and 138 € on extra-EU legs. FuelEU Maritime limits the greenhouse intensity of fuel; a ship running only on fossil fuel pays at most 62 € and 31 € per tonne. Together they equal about 51% of the fuel bill intra-EU and 22% on extra-EU legs (bunker-emissioni.json).

What carriers charge: Maersk's emissions surcharge for the fourth quarter is 170 $ per 40-foot container (FEU) from the Far East to North Europe, 4.7% of the Drewry Shanghai–Rotterdam rate of 3,615 $ (Maersk, 1 September 2026).

In the Asia–Rotterdam example fuel is 3.04 million €, the ETS 0.55 million, FuelEU at most 0.12 million and one war-risk premium 0.40–0.59 million: 4.21 million in total with the mid premium. The insurance figure is approximate: the last public Red Sea quote (0.2–0.3% of hull value, 24 September 2026) is for tankers without Saudi ties, and the hull value is the price of a new 15,500-TEU ship, a maximum.

War-risk insurance in the Baltic. The Joint War Committee of the London market lists all Russian ports, the Baltic ones included (Primorsk, Ust-Luga), as areas of heightened risk (circular JWLA-035 of 16 September 2026, assicurazioni.json): a ship calling there must notify its insurer and pay an additional premium. Missing: no public premium for Baltic calls was found. The coastal states face a different insurance risk: shadow-fleet tankers with unknown cover sail through Danish waters.

Freight. Drewry's index puts a 40-foot container from Shanghai at 3,615 $ to Rotterdam and 4,166 $ to Genoa (5 November 2026). The Freightos China–North Europe index (FBX11) is 3,422 $ (−4.8% on the week to 6 November 2026) and the Shanghai CCFI Europe index 1,860.07 points (+1.53%) (noli.json).

Transit times to Rotterdam

Ships rerouted around the Cape of Good Hope need roughly 7,000 km and 14–24 days more between Asia and Europe than via Suez (UNCTAD 2024, in stretti-dettaglio.json). In the week to 1 November 2026 the Suez Canal saw 43.0 transits a day (+10.3% on a year) and the Cape 92.0 (+5.0%) (portwatch.json). BIMCO counts 753,000 TEU a week of container capacity through Suez in early October 2026 against 1,457,000 in 2023: just over half of the pre-crisis level.

The monitor's routing model (transit-times.json, updated 10 November 2026) puts a ~15,000 TEU container ship from Shanghai (Yangshan) to Rotterdam at 29.6 days at sea via Suez today (10,674 nautical miles at 15 knots), and 40.1 days via the Cape, 10.5 days more. Sea time only: no canal waiting, port time or bunkering stops; distances are within 5% of published ones. The model's extra days via the Cape are fewer than UNCTAD's 14–24 because they are sea days at one fixed speed from one origin; UNCTAD's range covers several origins and speeds.

Drones

baltico-sicurezza.json records 15 drone events around North Sea and Baltic airports, bases, ports and energy sites since September 2025. Copenhagen airport closed for nearly four hours on 22 September 2025; drones flew over the Antwerp port area on 6 November 2025 and over the Doel nuclear plant on 9 November 2025; on 4 August 2026 a drone carrying explosives hit an Antonov An-124 at Leipzig. On 18 October 2026 Lithuania closed Kaunas airport after detecting an unidentified flying object. On 25 June 2026 Copenhagen police said they could neither prove nor rule out that drones had been there on 22 September 2025 (Wikipedia and sources cited, reliability medium): an alarm alone can shut a hub.

GPS jamming (GNSS interference)

GNSS satellite navigation signals are jammed or spoofed daily in the eastern Baltic. GPSJam, which reads the navigation quality reported by aircraft, showed 214 to 246 map cells a day at a high level (over 10% of aircraft affected) between 2 November and 8 November 2026. Almost all were east of 17°E (Kaliningrad, Lithuania, the Gulf of Finland, eastern Poland); one lay just west of it, at 55.8°N 16.96°E (10.7% of 681 aircraft). Aircraft data show where interference reaches, not where the jammers are, and say nothing directly about ships (baltico-sicurezza.json).

Shadow fleet in the Danish straits and the Gulf of Finland

The Danish Maritime Authority counted 292 passages of EU-sanctioned tankers through Danish waters in 2025, almost one a day (Euronews). PortWatch measures only the Øresund, not the Great Belt used by the largest tankers: there, 38.7 transits a day in the week to 1 November 2026, −20.5% on a year (tankers −18.9%) (porti-nord.json).

In the AIS snapshot of 9 November 2026 the monitor saw 109 ships in the Gulf of Finland: 44 on US, UK or EU sanction lists and 38 probable shadow fleet. Counting tankers only, 38 of 96 were sanctioned there and 7 more in the rest of the Baltic (navi.json, baltico-sicurezza.json). The Danish straits snapshot showed 27 ships and 1 sanctioned, but coverage there is thinner. Ships with AIS off are not counted.

Coastal states have become more active: baltico-sicurezza.json lists 6 detentions, boardings or refusals of entry in 2026, one of them by Russia. They include Swedish detentions of tankers off Gotland and Trelleborg and Germany's refusal of the tanker Tavian. Russia detained a Greek-owned tanker leaving an Estonian port on 26 July 2026. The Danish straits are rated «medium» on the risk dashboard (8.3 out of 25, from 5.8 a week earlier).

Cyber: on 29 December 2025 a destructive attack hit more than 30 wind and solar farms in Poland; the plants lost remote control and communication, but power output did not stop (cyber-energia.json).

7. Hydrogen and new fuels in ports: a reality check

Europe had 571 MW of electrolysers in operation in July 2025 (Hydrogen Europe, idrogeno.json), against the 90–100 GW the European Commission estimated for its 2030 goal of 10 million tonnes of domestic renewable hydrogen. Among the North-West European port projects tracked by the monitor, 300 MW operate, 525 MW are under construction, 450 MW are in development and 460 MW are suspended (a sample, not a census).

Costs. The IEA put hydrogen from offshore wind at 5.3–7 €/kg in 2024. Grey hydrogen made from gas would cost about 3.8 €/kg at today's TTF (82.33 €/MWh), an interpolation by the monitor that ignores the carbon price. Expensive gas narrows the gap but does not close it.

Pipes. The Delta Rhine Corridor from Rotterdam to Germany is now expected in 2031–2032, about four years late; until then hydrogen derivatives move by barge. Denmark's first hydrogen pipeline (Esbjerg–German border) is built only if the market books at least 0.5 GW by 1 December 2026 (Pipeline Journal). Germany's core network has its first operating stretches (Lubmin–Bobbau, Lingen–Marl).

Ship fuels. Rotterdam ran an 800 m³ ship-to-ship ammonia bunkering pilot in April 2025. In the third quarter of 2026 the port sold 23,613 m³ of bio-LNG and 4,166 t of bio-methanol, next to 215,420 m³ of fossil LNG (bunker-emissioni.json). Kassø in Denmark can make up to 42,000 t a year of e-methanol.

AssessmentBefore 2030 North Sea ports will be local hydrogen producers for refineries and developers of ammonia import terminals, not import hubs. The test to watch is commercial: offtake contracts and pipeline bookings, such as the Danish 1 December deadline, say more than new announcements.

Each file lists its sources, licences and the date of each datum; the text cites the date of the datum, not of the reading.

  • Coverage. PortWatch counts ships with AIS on; it sees about ten days late and undercounts Russian ports. Port tonnages are curated by hand from port authorities, some via trade press.
  • Seven days is too short. Weekly port-call changes swing by ±10–30% on small numbers; the report leads with 30-day and year-to-date averages.
  • Conflicting counts. baltico-sicurezza.json counts all sanctioned ships in the Gulf of Finland (44 of 109); navi.json counts tankers (38 of 96 sanctioned) and the risk dashboard counts sanctioned tankers in the whole Baltic (45). They are different perimeters of the same snapshot.
  • Offshore wind totals differ between WindEurope (national, end-2025) and EMODnet (map polygons, status sometimes late, duplicates removed).
  • Brent in prezzi.json is a provisional front-month close updated overnight; other files of the monitor quote an earlier reading. This report does not use Brent.
  • Missing: AGSI+ daily fill for Denmark and Poland; monthly LNG import volumes by country and origin; any public war-risk premium for Baltic calls.
  • Hand-written items: the external fact on the EU-wide US LNG share and the thresholds of section 8. Figures quoted from curated text fields of the files (for example the 292 tanker passages or the Fitburg sentence) are typed in the script and checked against the source text at every run; all other numbers are computed by report/build_northern_europe.py from the files above.
  • Different assumptions between files. The cost stack uses a 221.7 million $ hull (assicurazioni.json); transit-times.json uses 150 million $ for the same ship type, so its Red Sea premium is lower. Port-risk.json computes its own 7-day port-call changes, which can differ by a few tenths from porti-nord.json.

Glossary. AIS: transponder by which ships broadcast identity and position. TEU/FEU: 20-/40-foot container. TTF: Dutch gas hub price. LNG: liquefied natural gas. HVDC: high-voltage direct current. CfD: contract for difference. EUA: EU emission allowance (1 t CO₂e). FuelEU Maritime: EU rule on the greenhouse intensity of ship fuel. War risk: separate insurance for war, mines and seizure. Shadow fleet: old tankers with opaque ownership used to move sanctioned oil. GNSS: satellite navigation (GPS, Galileo).
