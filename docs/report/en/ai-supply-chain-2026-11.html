1. Summary

  • Beijing announces the rare-earth extension, with no official text. On 9 November China extended to 10 January 2027 the suspension of its October 2025 measures (Reuters); the text is not yet on the MOFCOM website. The ban on gallium and germanium exports to the US is suspended until 27 November 2026. China produces 99% of primary gallium and 69.2% of rare earths (USGS, 2025).
  • Three single points of failure hold the chain together. EUV scanners come only from Veldhoven (Netherlands); TSMC has 72.5% of world foundry revenue (Q2 2026, TrendForce) and makes 2 nm only in Taiwan; CoWoS packaging for GPUs is almost entirely in Tainan and Chiayi. Arizona, Kumamoto and Dresden will not change the picture before 2028.
  • Hormuz is almost closed; the Taiwan Strait is not. 4.4 ships a day passed through Hormuz in the week to 1 November 2026, −95% on the same week of 2025 (IMF PortWatch); Qatar, with 33.2% of world helium, halted production on 2 March (AP). The Taiwan Strait saw 247 ships a day, +7.1% on the same week of 2025.
  • Memory is the bottleneck that is tightening. HBM is split between SK Hynix 50%, Samsung 33% and Micron 18% (Counterpoint, Q2 2026); TrendForce expects DRAM contract prices +20% quarter on quarter in Q4 (TrendForce). For China, HBM is the ceiling on Huawei’s output.
  • On the stock market memory and equipment rise, while Nvidia stays below its start-of-year level. Year to date SK Hynix +87% (session of 10 November 2026, provisional), ASML +41%, SOX index +42%, AMD +137%; Nvidia −4.2% (closes of 9 November 2026, prezzi.json). Over the last month semiconductors have corrected: SOX −20%.
AssessmentThe AI chain is long but narrows at a few points, and almost all of them are in East Asia or depend on a single company. For Northern Europe the strongest tie is also the most solid: the EUV machines of Veldhoven. The weak points are upstream (rare earths, magnets, gallium, helium) and downstream (energy and transformers), more than in the chips.

Chinese deadlines: status at 10 November 2026

  • October 2025 measures (five more rare earths, the 0.1% rule, plants and technologies) — suspended until 10 January 2027 (in 61 days); extension announced, primary text not found (previous deadline: 10 November 2026)
  • The Chinese licences on gallium and germanium (2023), on the seven medium and heavy rare earths (April 2025) and the helium export ban (July 2026) are in force in any case.

3. Raw materials

Where it sits. China (gallium, rare earths, fluorspar, silicon), Chile and Congo (copper), Congo and Rwanda (tantalum), US and Qatar (helium), North Carolina (quartz), Ukraine (neon, until 2022).

Who controls it. The Chinese state, through export licences; a handful of companies for quartz (Sibelco, The Quartz Corp); QatarEnergy for helium; armed groups in Kivu for part of the coltan.

This week’s risk is Chinese controls. The licences on gallium and germanium (August 2023) and on the seven medium and heavy rare earths (April 2025) are in force and have never been suspended. The ban on exports of gallium, germanium and antimony to the US (December 2024) is suspended until 27 November 2026: no extension has been found. The October 2025 measures, including the rule on 0.1% Chinese content in foreign products, were suspended until 10 November 2026. On 9 November China’s Ministry of Commerce (MOFCOM) announced a two-month extension, to 10 January 2027 (Reuters, via The Star); Shanghai Metals Market reports its text as announcement no. 58 of 2026. Status: extension announced, not verified against the primary source: as of 10 November the announcement does not appear in the official MOFCOM list, which stops at no. 52 of 26 October. Tech Times, published on 9 November before the announcement, wrote that no text moved the date.

In depth (3 paragraphs)

An AI chip contains a few tens of grams of materials, but without some of them the fab stops. Gallium is used in power semiconductors and optics, germanium in fibre, helium to cool wafers, neon in lithography lasers, fluorspar in etching gases, Spruce Pine quartz in the crucibles where silicon is melted. Copper and rare earths are needed downstream: cables, transformers, fan and cooling-system motors, hard drives.

The concentration is extreme and can be measured with the latest edition of the USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026 (5 February 2026, 2025 data): the table below computes the shares from production data by country.

Helium is the textbook case of a logistics risk turning into an industrial one. Qatar produces a third of the world’s helium (33.2% in 2025, USGS) at Ras Laffan; QatarGas halted production on 2 March 2026 and declared force majeure two days later, because of the war with Iran (WTOP/AP, March 2026). The current state of production is not verified by a primary source; Hormuz remains closed in practice (portwatch.json). In June Morningstar/Dow Jones explained why the shock had not hit chipmakers: long-term contracts, diverse suppliers, storage caverns and on-site stocks. Since 10 July 2026 China too has temporarily banned helium exports (MOFCOM announcement no. 29), but it produces little (1.6% of the world total).

AssessmentFor Northern Europe the weak link is rare earths. The Baltic hosts the only complete stretch of a European supply chain outside China, at Sillamäe and Narva in Estonia, a few kilometres from the Russian border; Greenland (Denmark) has 1.5 million tonnes of reserves but no production.

Other materials under Chinese licensing touch the chain. China’s share of world output in 2025: magnesium metal 86.4%, tungsten 78.7%, natural graphite 77.8%, antimony 36.1% (minerali.json, USGS).

4. Chipmaking equipment

Where it sits. Netherlands (Veldhoven), Germany (Oberkochen, Ditzingen), California (San Diego, Santa Clara, Fremont, Milpitas), Japan (Tokyo).

Who controls it. ASML for lithography (the sole EUV supplier), Applied Materials, Lam Research, KLA and Tokyo Electron for the rest; the governments of the US, the Netherlands and Japan through licences.

This is the link where Europe matters most, and the most direct tie to this observatory’s field of work: every EUV scanner in the world comes out of Veldhoven, near Eindhoven. Inside are optics from Carl Zeiss SMT (Oberkochen, about a quarter owned by ASML), the CO2 laser from Trumpf (Ditzingen) and the light source from Cymer, an ASML company in San Diego (The Value Edge, an analysis generated with an AI assistant: low reliability). The EUV chain therefore runs through the Netherlands, Germany and the United States before reaching Taiwan, Korea, Japan and Arizona.

In depth (2 paragraphs)

In Q3 2026 ASML sold 113 new lithography systems (86 in Q2) for €11.7 billion of revenue; for the whole of 2026 it expects €44–45 billion (ASML press release of 14 October 2026, filed with the SEC). China buys only DUV and would account for about 20% of 2026 sales according to management (ai-mondo.json, transcript of the July call): the October release does not give the share.

The controls have built up in layers: the US in October 2022 and October 2023; the Netherlands from 1 September 2023 and from 7 September 2024 for the 1970i and 1980i immersion DUV systems; Japan from 23 July 2023 for 23 types of equipment (licence to all countries, simplified procedure for 42); the US on 2 December 2024 for 24 types of equipment, 3 software tools, HBM and 140 entities (BIS). In 2026 the open front is servicing of machines already installed in China, which The Hague still allows, and the US proposal (MATCH Act) to ban exports to China of all immersion DUV systems and to push allies to align (Bits&Chips).

AssessmentA halt to EUV would be the heaviest blow to the chain, but also the least likely because it involves allied countries. The concrete risk for Veldhoven is political and commercial (rules on DUV and on servicing in China), not physical.

5. Wafers and fabrication

Where it sits. Taiwan (Hsinchu, Kaohsiung, Taichung), Arizona, Japan (Kumamoto, Hokkaido), Korea (Pyeongtaek), China (Shanghai), Germany (Dresden), Ireland.

Who controls it. TSMC, by a wide margin; then Samsung, SMIC and Intel; Rapidus as a Japanese state-backed project.

TSMC had 72.5% of world foundry revenue in Q2 2026, Samsung 5.9%, SMIC 5.4% (TrendForce, 9 September 2026). At the leading edge the concentration is stronger: 2 nm has been in volume production only in Hsinchu and Kaohsiung since the end of 2025, and the AI chips of Nvidia, AMD, Broadcom and Google come off TSMC 3–4 nm lines. Shares by node from a verified source: not found.

In depth (2 paragraphs)

Diversification is real but slow. In Arizona TSMC produces at 4 nm, 3 nm will arrive in H2 2027 and the plan has grown to 265 billion dollars (BetaNews); but the American wafers go back to Taiwan for packaging. In Europe ESMC in Dresden will make 28–12 nm chips for cars, not for AI, and the tools will arrive only in H2 2027.

SMIC makes Huawei’s chips at 7 nm without EUV; its revenue grew 20% in the quarter. Nexperia (Nijmegen) shows the other side of European risk: a Dutch state intervention in September 2025 split the company in two, with the Chinese part in Dongguan.

Bottleneck: Leading-edge foundry concentrated in Taiwan. TSMC has 72.5% of foundry revenue and 2 nm is in volume only in Hsinchu and Kaohsiung; the Taiwan Strait is the single point of failure.

6. Advanced packaging and HBM memory

Where it sits. Taiwan (Tainan, Chiayi) for CoWoS; Korea (Icheon, Cheongju, Onyang) for HBM; Japan, Singapore and Indiana for upcoming memory capacity; Hefei for Chinese HBM.

Who controls it. TSMC for CoWoS; SK Hynix, Samsung and Micron for HBM; CXMT in China.

An AI GPU is not a chip but a package: TSMC’s logic die is mounted next to several stacks of HBM memory on an interposer (CoWoS). This step happens almost entirely in Taiwan. TSMC’s CoWoS capacity goes from 65,000–75,000 wafers a month in 2025 to a target of 120,000–130,000 in 2026; Nvidia has about 60% of the 2026 allocations, Broadcom about 15%, AMD about 11% (Tom’s Hardware, May 2026). For the end of 2027 KGI estimates 170,000 wafers a month.

In Q2 2026 HBM was split between SK Hynix 50%, Samsung 33% and Micron 18% (Counterpoint): Samsung gained twelve points in one quarter. AI is taking capacity away from ordinary DRAM: TrendForce expects DRAM contract prices +20% quarter on quarter in Q4 2026 (TrendForce, 20 October 2026).

In depth (1 paragraph)

For China HBM is the real constraint: the US controls of December 2024 cut off Korean memory, and according to Epoch AI Huawei will produce in 2026 less than 4% of Nvidia’s AI compute.

Bottleneck: CoWoS packaging. Every high-end GPU goes through TSMC’s CoWoS in Taiwan; Nvidia has booked about 60% of 2026 capacity. Packaging in Arizona will arrive from 2028.

Bottleneck: HBM and DRAM memory. Three producers (SK Hynix, Samsung, Micron) make all Western HBM; AI is taking capacity away from ordinary DRAM and prices are rising. SK Hynix sees a shortage until 2030.

7. Chip design and accelerators

Where it sits. California (Santa Clara, Palo Alto, Mountain View), Shenzhen.

9. Logistics

Where it sits. Taoyuan, Incheon, Schiphol, Anchorage and Pudong airports; the Taiwan Strait, the South China Sea, Malacca, Hormuz.

Who controls it. Cargo carriers (China Airlines, EVA Air, integrators), airports and, in a crisis, navies.

The Taiwan Strait is the single point of failure. 48% of the 5,400 container ships in service worldwide passed through it at least once between January and July 2022 (Bloomberg, cited in stretti-dettaglio.json). In Bloomberg Economics’ extreme scenario, a US–China war over Taiwan would cost the world economy about 10.6 trillion dollars (9.6% of world GDP) in the first year (Insurance Journal, February 2026). In a blockade, companies that depend on Taiwanese chips would lose up to 1.6 trillion dollars of revenue a year according to Rhodium (2022 estimate). The subsea cables around the island have already been damaged several times (cavi-incidenti.json).

In depth (2 paragraphs)

Chips and servers travel by air: they are worth a lot and weigh little. Machines travel dismantled: one EUV system needs about 40 containers, 20 trucks and 3 Boeing 747 freighters (Asia Times; DHL); a High-NA system went to Oregon in 43 containers. Materials (helium, quartz, LNG for Taiwan’s power plants) travel by sea.

Taiwan’s air cargo is driven by AI: for EVA Air AI-related freight is 40–50% of cargo, and North America about 70% of cargo revenue (Taiwanese press). Transpacific routes stop over in Anchorage.

AssessmentIn a naval blockade aircraft could keep carrying chips for a while, but LNG could not: Taiwan would stop for lack of energy before it stopped for lack of transport. Earthquakes (April 2024: TSMC recovered over 70% of its tools within 10 hours, Tom’s Hardware), drought and blackouts are the recurring risks.

10. Energy and data centres

Where it sits. United States (Texas, Tennessee, Indiana, Georgia), the Gulf, Malaysia (Johor), Europe (France, the Nordics, Italy); Taiwan for fab power.

Who controls it. The big clouds (Microsoft, Amazon, Google, Meta, Oracle), the labs (OpenAI, xAI, Anthropic as a customer), grid operators and power producers.

Energy enters the chain twice. Upstream, Taiwan generates half its electricity from gas and holds LNG stocks for 12–14 days (Focus Taiwan); it has shut its nuclear plants and is now considering restarting Maanshan (FDD). Downstream, US campuses run into transformer and grid-connection lead times, European ones into price: gas-fired projects such as Ferrera Erbognone tie Italian AI to TTF, while France leverages nuclear (the Fouju campus, 1.4 GW, is about 55 km from the Nogent-sur-Seine plant).

In depth (2 paragraphs)

According to the IEA data centres consumed about 485 TWh in 2025 (+17%) and will reach about 950 TWh in 2030, around 3% of world electricity; consumption at AI-dedicated sites grew 50% in 2025 (IEA, Key questions on energy and AI: page not reachable from here, figures taken from the IEA summary quoted in the press).

Money is not the problem: for 2026 Amazon indicates about 220 billion dollars of capital expenditure, Alphabet 195–205, Microsoft 170–190, Meta 135–145 (company guidance from late October, secondary sources disagree; investimenti.json). Most of it goes to the US.

AssessmentFor Northern Europe the advantage is clean electricity and a cold climate (the Nordics, Finland), the limit is grid capacity; the link to the map is in the power grid and interconnector layers.

Bottleneck: Transformers and grid connections. The money is there, the transformers are not: lead times for step-up transformers exceed three years and utilities buy five years ahead.

Bottleneck: Taiwan’s power supply for the fabs. Taiwan imports almost all its energy, generates half its electricity from gas and holds LNG stocks for less than two weeks: a naval blockade would hit the fabs without touching them.

Bottleneck: Energy cost for data centres. In Europe gas-fired projects (such as Ferrera Erbognone) tie AI to TTF and therefore to Hormuz; in the US what matters is the Henry Hub price and grid capacity.

11. Europe and Italy: where the chain touches them

  • Rare-earth magnets: the starkest dependency. In 2025 the EU bought from China 88.3% of the permanent magnets (NdFeB) imported from outside the Union, 1,061 million dollars out of 1,201; Italy 80.1% of its own (91.9 million dollars). These are the magnets in data-centre motors, fans, pumps and hard drives (commercio.json, UN Comtrade).
  • Rare earths upstream of the magnets. China accounts for 78.4% of the rare-earth metals imported by the EU in 2025; for separated oxides and compounds 39.2%, followed by Norway and Malaysia. Italian purchases are small and come through other EU countries (France, Austria, the Netherlands, Estonia), which customs data record as countries of consignment, not of origin.
  • Ultra-high-purity silicon: an American dependency, not a Chinese one. The EU imports 73.3% of its silicon above 99.99% from the US; Italy buys 58.3% of it from Denmark (country of consignment).
  • Veldhoven (ASML) is the most critical node of the chain located in Europe: the sole EUV maker, 10.8 km from a 380 kV line (rete-elettrica.json). The machines leave dismantled for Taiwan, Korea, Japan and the US; by air the natural exit is Schiphol (assessment: ASML does not publish which airports it uses).
  • Nexperia (Nijmegen) shows the opposite risk: the Dutch state’s takeover (September 2025) and the Chinese response broke a Europe–China chain. It does not affect AI chips, but it is the precedent to keep in mind for ASML.
  • Denmark and Greenland. Greenland has 1,500,000 tonnes of rare-earth reserves (USGS) and no production: a long-term option, not supply. The Danish Straits are not a route for the chip chain.
  • The Baltic. In Estonia, at Sillamäe (37.6 km from the 450 kV Estlink 2 HVDC line) and Narva (63.7 km from the 450 kV Estlink 2 HVDC line), Neo Performance Materials separates rare earths (including terbium and dysprosium from 2026) and makes magnets (2,000 t a year at start-up, 5,000 in prospect): the most complete stretch of a European supply chain outside China, a few kilometres from the Russian border. To watch: security of the border zone and of the Baltic cables (cavi-incidenti.json).
  • Italy. The Eni–Khazna campus at Ferrera Erbognone (500 MW in the first phase, gas-fired with CO2 capture) ties Italian AI to the TTF gas price and therefore to Hormuz (assessment).

13. Method, sources and limits

  • Data. The file docs/data/supply-chain-ai.json is built by raccolta/supply-chain-ai/raccogli.py from hand-curated entries (raccolta/supply-chain-ai/*.json), each with source, link, date and reliability, and from other observatory files (ai.json, ai-mondo.json, stretti-dettaglio.json, portwatch.json, prezzi.json, cavi-incidenti.json, rete-elettrica.json, cavi.json, minerali.json, commercio.json, investimenti.json). Country shares are computed from the USGS tables and checked against minerali.json; days to deadlines and indicator statuses are computed by the script as of 10 November 2026.
  • Dates. PortWatch: week to 1 November 2026. Prices: closes of 9 November 2026, unless stated. USGS shares: 2025 output (estimates), published on 5 February 2026. Trade: year 2025 (UN Comtrade).
  • Coordinates. New nodes are geocoded with OpenStreetMap Nominatim at the level of municipality, headquarters or site (precision in the file); those already on the map reuse the coordinates of ai.json and ai-mondo.json. The point for a headquarters is not the location of a plant.
  • Limits. Missing from a verified source: shares by technology node (3 nm, 2 nm), market shares of individual equipment suppliers, the current share of Ukrainian neon, AI cargo volumes by airport, the world share of germanium and polysilicon, the current state of helium production at Ras Laffan, the official text of China’s 9 November extension. Shares of AI chips (Nvidia, ASICs) and optical modules vary widely between sources. Secondary press sources and analyses generated with AI assistants have low reliability and are flagged.
  • Thresholds. Indicator thresholds are an observatory assessment: structural (concentration) indicators often remain “critical” and show where the chain would break, not that it is breaking.
  • This is not investment advice. Stocks are linked to the links of the chain to read the chain through the markets.
  • Use of an AI assistant and transparency. Collection of the entries, scripts, drafting and review were done with the help of an AI assistant (Claude, a model by Anthropic), under the care and responsibility of Gabriele Giuliani, who is accountable for the choices and assessments. Anthropic is a customer of this chain: it uses chips, servers and data centres described here (for example Amazon’s New Carlisle campus). Data concerning it come only from public sources and are treated like the rest.
