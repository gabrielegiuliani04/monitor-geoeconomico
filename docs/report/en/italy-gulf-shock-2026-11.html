1. Key points

  • Diesel has risen less than the end of the excise cut implies. The 4.9-cent cut expired on 8 November. Diesel averaged €2.238 a litre in the week of 2 November (MASE), +37% on a year; full excise would make it €2.287 (calculation). Self-service diesel rose only from €2.224 to €2.232 between 7 and 9 November (Mimit).
  • Gas and power bills now push inflation more than fuel does. Consumer prices (Istat NIC, preliminary) were up 5.4% on a year in October 2026, from 1.5% in February. Electricity (+35.8%) and gas (+35.6%) account for about 2.1 points, fuels for about 1.3 (calculation). The wholesale power price (PUN) averaged 230.8 €/MWh in October.
  • Italy has the gas; the problem is its price. Storage was 88.9% full on 8 November (Germany 58.9%). Algeria's TransMed pipeline, flowing normally, brought 34% of imported gas over 12 months; Qatar, in force majeure since March, supplied 32.6% of LNG imports by value in 2025. The Dutch TTF gas price, which Italy's follows, was +164% on a year on 9 November.
  • No national truck stoppage had been called by 9 November, and none can lawfully start before 5 December. Hauliers must give the Strike Guarantee Commission 25 days' notice. Diesel has been above the monitor's €2.10 alert level since the week of 24 August. Assessment: the risk that a stoppage is called before the end of the year is high; wildcat blockades need no notice.
  • A second Hormuz shock would put diesel at €2.64–2.98 within a month (assessment: Brent up 40–66% as in spring 2026, no new tax cuts). Tested on 2026: from pre-war prices the rule gave €1.96–2.21; diesel averaged €2.11 once the excise cuts are added back. A TransMed outage would hit bills, not pumps.

2. Where Italy's energy comes from and what the Gulf crisis changed

Italy imports 73.9% of the energy it uses, 95.2% of its gas and 89.8% of its oil (net imports over gross supply, 2024, Eurostat via paesi.json). Fuels (HS chapter 27) were 10.7% of the value of everything Italy imported in 2025, 71.5 billion dollars (UN Comtrade, commercio.json).

Gas: six pipelines, five LNG terminals

In the 12 months to 8 November 2026 the largest entry point was Mazara del Vallo, where Algerian gas arrives through the TransMed pipeline system (several parallel lines under the Sicily Channel): 220.5 TWh, 34.0% of all gas entering by pipe and ship. Next came the TAP pipeline from Azerbaijan at Melendugno (16.5%) and the five LNG terminals together (32.7%; Adriatic LNG alone 14.1%). Residual Russian gas through Tarvisio was 2.7% (ENTSOG daily physical flows, gas-italia.json). National production and storage are not in these shares.

Two movements stand out. OLT, the floating terminal off Livorno, delivered 0.8 TWh in October against 4.0 in September; it restarted on 8 November (105 GWh). Mazara dipped to 11.3 TWh in September, from 16.9 in August, and recovered to 20.2 in October. Missing: the cause of either change is not in the data.

LNG from Qatar. In 2025 the United States supplied 45.3% of the value of Italy's LNG imports, Qatar 32.6% and Algeria 10.8% (UN Comtrade, paesi.json). QatarEnergy has been in force majeure since 4 March 2026; for the cargoes due to Edison (the Adriatic LNG terminal) it was extended to the end of December (gasworld), and Iranian missile attacks knocked out about 17% of Qatar's export capacity; Qatar's LNG exports were 1.6 million tonnes in September, under a quarter of the pre-war average, and on track for 0.6 million in October (Kpler) (Reuters via Cyprus Mail, 12 October 2026; the monitor's Hormuz report identifies the damaged units as trains 4 and 6, about 12.8 Mt a year). The monitor's risk dashboard rates Qatari LNG critical, 24.0 out of 25 (rischio.json, 9 November 2026). Missing: the origin of the cargoes that reached Italian terminals in 2026 (it would need Kpler or GIIGNL data).

TransMed. The same dashboard rates the pipeline low at 5.6 out of 25 (probability 1.3 of 5, impact 4.3 of 5), down from 8.0 a week earlier. Flows were normal: 620.7 GWh a day over the last seven days against a 12-month average of 602.7. It is Italy's largest single gas dependency, not its most likely disruption.

Between 5.6% and 10.2% of Italy's crude came from the Gulf in 2025 (the lower figure counts only suppliers with no route around Hormuz; the higher one adds Saudi Arabia and the UAE). The exposure is through price and refined products. The president of the fuel industry association Unem said in April that falls in diesel prices would prove temporary, citing among other things the closure of more than 30 European refineries in fifteen years (Il Fatto Quotidiano, 17 April 2026; reliability medium).

Emergency stocks: oil below the norm, gas close to target

Italy's emergency oil stocks covered 77.2 days in August 2026, against 90.5 a year earlier and a five-year average of 89.3 (Eurostat, scorte.json). EU law (directive 2009/119/EC) requires the larger of 90 days of net imports or 61 days of consumption. The series fell from 90.2 days in March to 76.7 in April 2026. Germany held 97.1, France 96.5 and the Netherlands 95.5 days. The June figure (89.7, between 76.6 in May and 77.3 in July) is almost certainly a reporting anomaly and is not used. Missing: whether the spring drawdown was part of a coordinated stock release (the Hormuz report suggests it probably was).

Gas storage was 88.9% full on 8 November 2026 (180.9 TWh of about 203.4), −4.7 points on a year earlier and −5.4 on the five-year average; winter withdrawals began on 1 November and took 0.23 points on the last day. Germany was at 58.9%, the Netherlands 63.5%, Austria 64.3% and France 80.8% (GIE AGSI+, scorte.json). For the IEA, EU storage enters the winter at its lowest level since 2013 (IEA, 3 November 2026).

AssessmentItaly's physical position is better than northern Europe's: full storage, two large pipelines from the south and five LNG terminals. Its weak points are the price, which follows the Dutch TTF, and the oil stocks, which were drawn down in spring and have not been rebuilt. A cold winter would test Germany and Austria first; Italy would feel it through the price.

3. At the pump and in the bill

Diesel and petrol

On 2 November 2026 a litre of diesel cost €2.238 on average: €1.202 for the fuel itself, €0.6329 of excise and €0.4036 of VAT. Petrol cost €2.001 (net €0.967, excise €0.6729) (MASE weekly national average, italia-impatti.json). The MASE average mixes self-service and attended pumps.

That survey still included the last diesel excise cut, 4.9 cents a litre with VAT, in force from 28 October to 7 November and not renewed (Rai News, 8 November 2026). With the ordinary excise of €0.6729 and an unchanged net price, diesel comes to €2.287 (calculation, mixed self-service and attended). The daily readings after the expiry show a much smaller rise: the Mimit self-service average on ordinary roads was €2.232 for diesel on 9 November, against €2.224 on 7 November, the last day of the cut; petrol was €2.000 (ANSA; Rai News). The ministry credits the voluntary price caps: since 28 September Eni/Enilive and IP have capped prices at about 4,100 of 20,000 stations (diesel €2.19, petrol €1.99; La Prealpina, 28 September 2026), and by 9 November, according to the ministry, the main companies had joined, keeping self-service diesel below €2.20 on most of the network. Assessment: the €2.29 estimate is an upper bound while the caps last.

In the last week before the war (23 February 2026) diesel cost €1.702 and petrol €1.655. Diesel peaked at €2.351 in the week of 28 September 2026, the highest weekly average in the file's series (which starts in January 2022). Since February the net price of diesel has risen 66%, while Brent crude in euro rose 37% (from a pre-war weekly average of 71.27 to the 9 November close of 92.57 dollars a barrel; calculation on italia-impatti.json and Yahoo Finance).

Since January 2026 diesel and petrol carry the same ordinary excise (€0.6729). On 2 November diesel cost 23.8 cents more than petrol, against 4.7 cents before the war, even though its excise was 4 cents lower that week. Before tax the gap is 23.5 cents, against 3.9 before the war (calculation).

AssessmentDiesel has risen about twice as fast as crude because the refining margin on diesel widened: Europe imports diesel, and part of it came from Gulf refineries. That is why haulage, which runs on diesel, is under more pressure than private motoring.

How crude reaches the pump. At constant refining margins, 10 dollars a barrel on Brent is worth 6.8 cents a litre with VAT (at 1.1225 dollars per euro). Estimated on the Italian data, the net price of diesel moves 1.40 times the change in Brent after 4 weeks, 1.66 after 8 and 1.88 in the long run; for petrol 1.03, 1.15 and 1.40. About 35% of the long-run effect on diesel passes in one week, 74% in four. Excise cuts pass faster but unevenly: in the first three days of the March 2026 cut only 60% of stations applied it (Mimit observatory via Today.it). These are the monitor's regressions (fit R² 0.52 at 4 weeks), reliability medium.

Electricity: the PUN

The PUN averaged 230.82 €/MWh in October 2026 and 277.29 in the first 10 days of November (293.86 on 10 November). Annual averages were 303.9 in 2022, 127.2 in 2023, 108.5 in 2024 and 115.9 in 2025 (GME, italia-impatti.json). Gas sets the power price in most hours: on 18 monthly and annual points the monitor fits PUN ≈ 47.8 + 1.96 × TTF (R² 0.95), so 10 €/MWh on gas adds about 20 on power. With October's TTF of 86.8 the formula gives 218, against an actual 231.

Regulated gas

For 'vulnerable' households (the elderly, the disabled, people on social bonuses and others still on regulated terms) ARERA sets the gas cost as the monthly average of the Italian wholesale price, so wholesale changes reach the bill in full a month later. The October 2026 value was 88.36 €/MWh, against 46.01 in April and 46.89 in May; the typical customer's bill rose 9.1% on September (ARERA, 3 November 2026), and the reference price was 176.80 cents per cubic metre (Sky TG24, not reachable from here). About 2.3 million households are still on this service (ARERA). Rule of thumb: 10 €/MWh on wholesale gas adds 10.6 cents per cubic metre plus VAT, about 7% of the bill. Missing: the June and August values.

On 9 November the cabinet approved a decree on energy bills: a cap of €58.9/MWh on the gas used to generate electricity, from January 2027 for 18 months, which the energy minister says will cut the wholesale power price by about 15 €/MWh on average (ANSA; Corriere della Sera). It applies to customers on variable-price power contracts, almost 18 million according to the Corriere. Missing: the published text of the decree and an official estimate of the saving per household.

4. Inflation and households

Istat's preliminary estimate puts consumer price inflation (NIC) at 5.4% in October 2026, the highest since August 2023 (5.4%); the 2022 peak was 11.8% in October 2022. The EU-harmonised index (HICP) was 5.6% (Istat and Eurostat, italia-impatti.json). Energy items rose 31.4%: regulated energy 59.5% (from 25.8% in September), electricity 35.8%, gas 35.6%, fuels 27.0%. Food rose 2.7% and core inflation (excluding energy and fresh food) 2.0%.

Energy weighs 10.9% of the 2026 HICP basket (fuels 4.7%, electricity 2.8%, gas 3.1%) and food 20.9% (Eurostat). A 10% rise in consumer energy prices therefore adds about 1.1 points of inflation directly. Weight times annual rate gives energy about 3.4 of the 5.6 points of HICP inflation in October: electricity and gas about 2.1, fuels about 1.3 (weights × NIC annual rates; approximation, calculation).

AssessmentMore than half of Italian inflation is the direct effect of energy. Indirect effects (transport, food) come later; food inflation has started to rise (1.0% in August, 2.7% in October) and freight transport services in the HICP rose 3.2% in September 2026.

Consumer confidence (Istat, 2021 = 100) was 90.3 in October 2026, against 97.4 in February; the judgement on the economy fell to 82.9, close to April's 82.7. The low of 2022 was 80.9 (October 2022). The file holds the series not adjusted for season; Istat's press release uses the adjusted one, which can differ.

Fuel sales. Road diesel sales fell 7.5% in September 2026 on a year earlier and diesel sold off the retail network (to fleets and hauliers) 9.4%; in May the falls were 10.8% and 14.3%. Petrol sales rose 4.9% (MASE, italia-impatti.json). Diesel was already falling and petrol rising in January and February, before the war.

AssessmentThree things are mixed in the diesel figures: a long shift of the car fleet from diesel to petrol and hybrids (visible before the war), weaker economic activity, and genuine saving. The literature puts the short-run price elasticity of petrol demand at −0.09 (Havranek, Irsova and Janda, 2012): a 10% price rise cuts consumption by about 1%. Off-network diesel, the hauliers' fuel, falling faster than road diesel points to less freight moved.

The IMF expects Italian growth of 0.8% and average inflation of 3.3% in 2026 (World Economic Outlook, October 2026, paesi.json). For the ECB, a geopolitical supply shock that raises the real oil price by 10% lowers euro-area growth by about 0.2–0.3 points in each of the first three years (ECB Economic Bulletin 4/2026).

Queues and empty shelves. In 2026 the queues and 'sold out' signs at filling stations came from price changes, not shortages: after the March excise cut, drivers rushed to the stations that had applied it; on 28 September they rushed to the Eni and IP stations with capped prices (sources in the precedents of italia-impatti.json). Food hoarding is documented only when trucks stopped: Sicily in January 2012 (water, milk) and March 2022 (pasta, flour, oil, sugar, rice).

5. Road haulage and the risk of a stoppage

How much diesel weighs

There is no up-to-date official Italian figure for the share of diesel in a haulier's costs (the association Federtrasporti noted the gap in September). The closest reference is French: diesel is 20.9% of the cost of a long-haul articulated truck in 2026, after 22.1% in 2025 and 24.4% in 2024 (Comité national routier, CNR; reliability medium: read from a research summary, the PDF is blocked from this environment and was not verified). The CNR estimated that the March 2026 rise alone added 4.2% to the total cost (CNR, 27 March 2026). Excluding VAT, which firms recover, diesel has gone from €1.395 to about €1.875 a litre since February (+34%); with the French weight that is roughly +7% on total cost. For a truck that gets the excise refund the litre has gone from €1.125 to €1.605 net of tax and refund (+43%), about +9% on total cost (calculation; the refund makes the percentage rise larger, because the excise it leaves is fixed).

The associations' own figures: about €100 a day and €2,000 a month more for a firm with one heavy truck (CNA Fermo, Logisticamente.it, October 2026); up to 40 cents a litre of extra burden and an average loss of about €9,000 a month per firm (Unatras, Il Fatto Quotidiano, 17 April 2026). These are claims by parties to the dispute.

The excise refund, and why tax cuts annoyed hauliers

Hauliers with heavy trucks get back part of the excise on diesel: the refund covers the excise above a fixed reference rate of €403.22 per 1,000 litres (art. 24-ter of the excise code; trucks of 7.5 tonnes or more, Euro V or cleaner). At the ordinary excise that is 27.0 cents a litre (26.5 according to Federtrasporti). Because the haulier's own excise is fixed at the reference rate, a general cut shrinks the refund by the same amount, as long as the cut is smaller than the refundable part. In April Unatras put about 20 of its 40 cents of extra burden down to this lost refund and asked for compensation of 200 euros per 1,000 litres (Il Fatto Quotidiano, 17 April 2026); in May it called the general cut 'a wrong decision that shifted the 20-cent reduction onto haulage firms' (Shipping Italy, 23 May 2026). It was one of the two grievances behind the stoppage called for 25–29 May; the other was clients' pressure on rates.

The government suspended that stoppage on 22 May with a package: a tax credit on diesel of 300 million euros (200 of them new), the quarterly excise refund paid in 30 days instead of 60, a two-week extension of the excise cut to 6 June, and a standing table with the sector. By November 242.2 million of the 480 million allocated had been granted, to be used by 31 December 2026 (Ministry of Transport via TrasportoEuropa, 9 November). On 21 October Unatras said firms had not yet received the credit and put the funds allocated at 434 million (Logisticamente.it). Both figures are allocations: the sources do not explain the 46-million difference (a later top-up is possible but not documented).

AssessmentFor a truck that qualifies for the refund, a general excise cut of up to about 27 cents is worth nothing in final cost: the price at the pump falls, the refund falls with it (VAT is recovered anyway). It only reduces the cash the firm advances until the quarterly refund. By the same logic the expiry of the cut on 8 November raises motorists' costs, not hauliers' final costs. The grievances that move hauliers are the tax credit, which must be used by 31 December, and clients' pressure on rates, not the expiry itself.

The rules on stoppages

Law 146 of 1990, amended in 2000, governs strikes in essential public services: notice, duration, minimum services, conciliation. The Strike Guarantee Commission reviews each call, can ask for it to be withdrawn and can refer it for a precettazione, an order by the minister or the prefect that requires the service to run (Normattiva). For haulage stoppages the sector's self-regulation code requires 25 days' notice to the Commission (the law sets a minimum of 10) and minimum services; a rule of 'objective spacing' forbids stoppages in the same sector too close together. In March 2022 and April 2026 the Commission challenged stoppages called with less than 25 days' notice (Trasportounito gave 20 days in April 2026, according to Unatras; LaPresse, 20 April 2026). The one national stoppage called with proper notice in 2026 (Unatras, 25–29 May, later suspended) was for five days; the maximum duration allowed by the code is not verified (the Commission's site was not reachable from this environment).

What happened: in December 2007 the stoppage lasted three days, with milk, medicines and petrol guaranteed from 12 December; in January 2012 the Forconi blockades left most of Sicily's filling stations dry by the fourth day, and about 60 pickets then blocked motorway toll gates across Italy; in March 2022 a stoppage was blocked by the Commission for lack of notice, and the excise cut followed on 22 March; in April 2026 Trasportounito's stoppage was suspended on its first day after a truck driver was killed on the A1 motorway; in May Unatras's was suspended after the government package; in October regional stoppages ran in Sicily and Calabria (16–20 October, in the ministry's strike calendar) after protests and blockades that began in Puglia.

AssessmentThe monitor's thresholds, built on these precedents, rate the risk low with diesel below €1.90 and an 8-week rise below 8%, medium between €1.90 and €2.10 or a rise of 8–15%, and high from €2.10 or a rise above 15%. Protests started at or near a new price peak and, in every haulage case, with diesel 16–50% above a year earlier. The November supply-chain report uses €1.90 and €2.20; the upper level here is lower because March 2022 and April 2026 started below €2.20.

Where things stand

No national haulage stoppage had been called in the sources consulted on 9 November (Rai News list of 8 November; the Ministry of Transport's strike calendar was not reachable from this environment). Unatras raised the possibility in October, and a meeting at the ministry was set for 27 October; its outcome was not found. Other national actions this month: a general strike by the CGIL union on 20 November and rail strikes, including freight, from 21:00 on 20 November to 20:00 on 21 November (Rai News, 8 November 2026). With 25 days' notice, a stoppage called on 10 November could not start before 5 December 2026.

Against the thresholds: diesel at €2.238 (about €2.29 after the expiry) is in the high band, where it has been since the week of 24 August 2026; the 8-week change is +4.5% (from €2.142 on 7 September), in the low band; the change on a year is +37.1%, inside the range of past protests.

AssessmentThe level of diesel alone has not been enough: it has been above €2.10 for more than two months without a national call. The 2026 calls came from a policy grievance on top of the price (the refund cut in May, unpaid credits in October). The risk that a national stoppage is called before the end of the year is rated high; a lawful stoppage before 5 December is ruled out by the notice rule. Wildcat blockades, like Sicily in 2012 or the protests in Puglia in October 2026, need no notice and remain possible at any time. Signals that would raise the risk: a filing with the Commission; credits not usable by 31 December; a new general excise cut without compensation for hauliers; diesel up more than 8% in 8 weeks. Signals that would lower it: the credit extended into 2027, or an automatic fuel surcharge on haulage rates, which Federtrasporti has proposed.

6. Ports and freight

From 1 January to 30 October 2026 the busiest Italian ports in the PortWatch sample had about the same number of ship calls as a year earlier: Genoa −0.4%, Trieste −0.6%, Gioia Tauro +5.0% (calls per day, same days of 2025; IMF PortWatch, portwatch.json). The largest falls are in the northern Adriatic: Ravenna −8.8% for the year and −20.0% in the last 30 days, Venice (Lido) −7.4% and −10.6%. La Spezia is up, +22.9% for the year.

The numbers are small: Ravenna had 6.4 calls a day in the last 30 days against 8.0 a year earlier, with tankers at 1.6 against 2.0. Weekly figures swing widely; the year-to-date figure is the one to lead with.

Transit times and costs to Genoa and Trieste

A 15,000-TEU container ship from Shanghai takes 24.4 days to Genoa via Suez and 40.1 via the Cape of Good Hope (15.7 more); to Trieste 24.1 and 42.8 (18.7 more). Northern Europe gains less from Suez: Shanghai–Rotterdam is 29.6 days against 40.1. With 63% of Far East–Europe capacity back on Suez services in October, the weighted average is 30.2 days to Genoa and 31.0 to Trieste (model in transit-times.json: sea days at a fixed speed, no port time).

Spot freight from Shanghai to Genoa was 4,166 dollars per FEU on 5 November 2026, against 3,615 to Rotterdam (Drewry WCI, noli.json); the Freightos China–Mediterranean index was 4,324 on 6 November (−1.17% on the week). The Red Sea war-risk premium, about 0.25% of hull value per voyage (Reuters via Insurance Journal), adds 262,500 dollars to a Suez voyage compared with before the war; with dearer bunker fuel the model puts the extra cost at about 232 dollars per FEU to Genoa.

For energy cargoes the routes have changed more. The model treats LNG from Ras Laffan as blocked: Qatar has no export terminal outside the Gulf, and the few cargoes still leaving (1.6 million tonnes in September, Kpler via Reuters) cross Hormuz under war risk. Saudi crude is piped to Yanbu on the Red Sea and reaches Genoa in 7.0 days via Suez, inside the war-risk area. LNG from the US Gulf (Sabine Pass) takes 13.8 days to Genoa (transit-times.json).

La Spezia's exposure

In the monitor's Port Risk Index La Spezia scores 7.2 out of 25 (medium), rank 7 of 39 ports and the highest in Italy (next is Livorno at 5.4) (port-risk.json, 10 November 2026). The score is probability 2.0 of 5 times impact 3.6 of 5. Impact is high because the index counts three strategic roles (the Panigaglia LNG terminal, a container gateway, the naval base). Probability comes almost entirely from a rule that gives the port a primary dependency on Qatari LNG and on the Red Sea container route, not from its traffic, which is rising: 2.2 calls/day vs 1.8 a year earlier over 28 days (+26.0%).

AssessmentThe index describes a structural exposure rather than a current disruption. Panigaglia is a small terminal: it delivered 9.8 TWh in 12 months, 1.5% of gas entering Italy (ENTSOG). The Qatar dependency is a rule that applies Italy's national share of LNG from the Gulf (32.6% by value in 2025) to every Italian LNG port; it is not an observed flow, since the repository does not show where Panigaglia's cargoes come from. La Spezia's score would fall mainly if the Qatari LNG and Red Sea scores in the risk dashboard fell; a collapse in its own calls (below half of last year's level) would raise it.

7. Scenarios: what a new shock would do

AssessmentEverything in this section is the monitor's reasoning. The scenarios have no probabilities. They show what the transmission rules give when a shock of a known shape hits Italian prices, with no new government measures and excise unchanged.

Method. The changes in Brent and TTF come from the monitor's simulator: for Hormuz, the average and the peak change in the 2026 episode over 30 or 91 days; for the Red Sea, the 2023–24 episode; for TransMed, the loss of the 20.86 billion cubic metres it carried in the last 12 months, net of 7.9 of spare LNG terminal capacity (low end) or with no bypass (high end). Italian prices then follow the rules of section 3 (italia-impatti.json, field scenari). There are two starting points: pre-war prices (week of 23 February 2026: Brent 71.27 dollars, TTF 31.59 €/MWh, diesel €1.702) and today's prices (Brent 91.7, TTF 82.33, diesel €2.287 after the expiry). The simulator's Brent base, 91.70, is an overnight price of 10 November, not the 9 November close of 92.57; the difference moves the diesel results by less than half a cent. Because Hormuz is already closed in practice, the 'today' version is a second shock on top of the first.

Backtest on February–March 2026

Applied to the prices of 23 February 2026, the rule gave diesel at €1.96–2.21 after one month of Hormuz. What happened (MASE, 4 weeks to 1 April): an average of €1.990 and a peak of €2.033 with the excise cuts, €2.112 and €2.277 without them (observed net price plus the pre-war excise, with VAT). At three months the rule gave €2.03–2.28; without the cuts diesel averaged €2.221 and peaked at €2.397 on the week of 13 April: the average falls inside the range, the peak above it.

Inflation rose from 1.5% in February to 1.7% in March, 2.7% in April and 3.0% in June: +1.2 and +1.5 points, against a direct effect of +1.4 to +2.3 (1 month) and +1.4 to +2.5 (3 months) in the rule, which ignores the excise cuts. The PUN was overestimated: the rule gave 156–174 €/MWh, March averaged 143.4.

For the Red Sea, the 2023–24 episode: diesel was €1.754 in the week of 11 December 2023 and averaged €1.728 in the next 4 weeks; the small rise the rule implies did not happen.

AssessmentThe rule is good enough for orders of magnitude on fuel at one to three months, underestimates the peak at three months and overestimates power in spring, when solar and hydro cut the hours in which gas sets the price. Excise cuts weigh heavily on the outcome: in 2026 they took 12–19 cents off the average diesel price.

Likely government measures

  • Hormuz closed, 1 month: an excise cut on fuel (2022: 30.5 cents with VAT; 2026: 24.4); a tax credit and faster excise refunds for hauliers (2026: 300 million euros); pressure for voluntary price caps (Eni and IP, September 2026).
  • Hormuz closed, 3 months: repeated extensions of the excise cut in decreasing amounts (2026); tax credits for energy-intensive firms and larger social bonuses on bills (2022); decrees on bills (November 2026: −15 €/MWh from January); aid to hauliers to head off a stoppage (May 2026).
  • Red Sea: ships via the Cape, 1 month: no fuel measures in 2023–24; possible support to ports.
  • TransMed shut, 1 month: maximum use of the LNG terminals; tax credits for energy- and gas-intensive firms (2022); larger social bonuses on power and gas (2022); a possible cut in the system charges on bills (2022).

Measures are drawn from what governments did in 2022 and 2026 (italia-impatti.json, field misure_governo). Assessment: in the TransMed case Italy's storage, at about 89%, makes it a problem of price, not quantity, unless the winter is cold; the energy-intensive industries (paper, ceramics, glass, steel) would be hit first. In the Hormuz cases, physical fuel shortages are unlikely in the first month thanks to emergency stocks, but queues appear wherever prices or excise change. Shortages on shelves have come only with truck stoppages (2012, 2022).

Limits.

  • The Mimit daily price file could not be downloaded from this environment; the report uses the MASE weekly average (self-service and attended together) and one daily figure from ANSA.
  • The pass-through coefficients are simple regressions estimated by the data collector on 304 MASE weeks from 11 January 2021 to 2 November 2026, a period with two large crises. The file keeps only the 253 weeks from January 2022, so the coefficients cannot be re-estimated from the repository alone (the MASE file has the missing weeks). They work for large shocks, less for small moves, and treat rises and falls alike, whereas pump prices usually fall more slowly than they rise.
  • Inflation in the scenarios is the direct effect of energy only. The ratio from the PUN to household power prices (0.75) comes from a single year, 2022 (reliability low).
  • The stoppage thresholds are a judgement on precedents, not a model. The diesel weight in haulage costs is French (CNR, long-haul articulated truck); it was read from a summary, not the PDF, and Italian trucks, wages and tolls differ.
  • Brent: prezzi.json records 91.70 dollars under 9 November 2026 (−6.7% on the day), but that is a price read at 03:40 UTC on 10 November, during the next session's overnight trading. The 9 November close was 92.57 (Yahoo Finance, read 10 November, −5.8% on the day); the risk dashboard and the Hormuz report use 92.62, an intraday reading of the same session. This report quotes the close; the scenarios keep the simulator's 91.70. The TTF close was 82.50 against 82.33 in the file; the +164% on a year is unchanged.
  • The haulage tax credit appears as 480 million euros allocated (Ministry of Transport, 9 November) and as 434 million allocated (Unatras, 21 October): the sources do not explain the difference.
  • The emergency-stock series has an anomalous June 2026 value (89.7 days between 76.6 and 77.3), not used. The diesel prices at the start of past protests were recomputed from the MASE weekly series by the review; the curated values in the data file use inconsistent reference weeks. Consumer confidence is not seasonally adjusted. Comtrade shares are by value and rely on the importer's declaration.
  • PortWatch counts only ships with AIS on and arrives a week late. The transit-time model has no port or waiting time.
  • Missing: an official Italian figure for diesel in haulage costs; the text of the haulage self-regulation code (maximum durations); the official strike calendar; the outcome of the 27 October meeting; the origin of LNG cargoes in 2026; the ARERA values for June and August 2026; the text of the 9 November bill decree.
